# Rubber Tower: layout sketch critique → "climb through a world" (prompt)

Paste the block into Claude Code.

```
STOP building the current layout. The sketch is decent, but it's a classic linear obby: a line of numbered hops up one side. I want it to feel like you're CLIMBING THROUGH A WORLD, exploring a fantasy valley as you go up, not hopping dots. Rework the layout first, then show me before building.

Read first: [[Rubber-Tower-Reference-Obbies]] (the chained-together takeaways: themed objects as platforms, the world feels like a place you travel through), [[Difficulty-And-Mastery]], [[Onboarding-And-First-60-Seconds]], [[Obby-Special-Platforms]], [[Rubber-Tower-Valley-Kit]].

WHAT'S WRONG WITH THE SKETCH
- One thin route up the west side, the rest of the valley is just decoration.
- The kit models sit next to the path instead of BEING the path.
- It's a dotted line: no places, no moments, nothing to discover.
- The special pieces are barely there (a couple of bounces). Where are the wobble, ice, soft, movement and climbing pieces?

WHAT I WANT
1. The models ARE the obby. Climb through and over the world:
   - up and over the shop rooftops and chimneys, up the windmill (inside or around its blades), up a giant tree / the fairy treehouse, across the pond on wobbly lily pads, along cliff ledges past the waterfall, through a cave/tunnel in the cliff, across the rope bridge, up the scaffold and the spiral steps;
   - floating islands, crystals and mushrooms as stepping stones, not just props.
2. Use the WHOLE valley: the route wraps around the valley (walls, centre, floating islands), crossing over itself at different heights so you see where you've been and where you're going. From each area you should see the next landmark and other players above/below you.
3. Areas, not dots. Break S1 into 4–6 small themed "places" with a moment each (e.g. Village Rooftops → Windmill Climb → Lily Pond → Waterfall Ledges → Treetop Path → CP1). Each place teaches, tests or twists one thing.
4. All the unique pieces, introduced properly (teach → test → twist, the sawtooth from [[Difficulty-And-Mastery]]):
   - bounce (mushrooms, flower pads, jelly, spring pads, leaves),
   - wobble (lily pads, jelly cubes, seesaw log, raft),
   - ice (a frosty patch / frozen puddle teaser in S1; it becomes the main thing in S3),
   - soft rests (hay bales, moss cushions, pillows, clouds) after hard bits,
   - movement (rope bridge, tightrope, swing paddles/hammers, windmill blades, fan vents, slingshot),
   - climbing (vine walls, ladders, shelf fungus, mushroom steps, scaffold, spiral steps).
   Plan which pieces S2–S4 bring back and twist, so S1 isn't using everything up.
5. Exploration and secrets: 1–2 optional side paths per area (a harder shortcut or a scenic detour) that rejoin the main route, hidden rubber ducks (Duck Whisperer), the secret room tucked off the route, and goofy little details (silly signposts, critters, a scarecrow) to find along the way.
6. Keep the rules: default movement, every jump fair on a phone, the next platform/landmark always visible, first jump easy and first fall within ~20 s lands soft (onboarding note), 5–8 minutes for S1 (dev timer), checkpoint tags/heights unchanged, the Leap of Faith lane and splash pond clear at every height, shops placed on the ground floor.

PROCESS
- Redo the layout as: a top-down map + side view of the WHOLE tower route (S1 detailed, S2–S4 rough so S1 fits the big picture), plus a short "player's-eye" storyboard: 5–6 moments in S1 described in one line each (what you see, what you do, which piece).
- Give me 2 layout options if you have different ideas.
- Stop and wait for my pick. Then build ground floor + S1, audit, timer, 4 angles + a short fly-through of the route, honest self-critique, and stop again.
- Log this critique in [[Rubber-Tower]] and [[Rubber-Tower-Map-Build-Plan]].
```
